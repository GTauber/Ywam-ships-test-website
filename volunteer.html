Your skill has a place on board.

Doctors and deckhands, welders and baristas: the m/v YWAM Liberty runs on volunteers. From her home port in Madang she brings free medical care to isolated islands and shoreline villages.

Everyone on board is a volunteer.

Patients pay nothing for their care. Volunteers cover their own costs, and every staff member raises their own support, from our founder to our janitor.

Where you'll serve

On the m/v YWAM Liberty, based in Madang, Papua New Guinea. Dental and eye-surgery clinics are usually on board. Primary care and optometry clinics are held in villages: in a school, a church, outside or in someone's home. General volunteers can also serve at Port YWAM, our base in Kona, Hawaiʻi.

How long

Medical outreaches run in cycles of about two weeks, and you're welcome to register for more than one. General volunteers join us in Kona for four weeks to three months. Our long-term staff have all completed a Discipleship Training School.

What it costs

Applying costs $50, plus a 2.3% surcharge. Each outreach has a fee that covers your food, cabin and the ship's upkeep. For outreaches in Papua New Guinea, a deposit is due within a week of your place being confirmed and the rest on arrival, in US dollars.

Your outreach fee covers

  • Food and accommodation
  • Airport pick-up and drop-off
  • Your outreach uniform: one collared YWAM shirt (two for marine crew)
  • Administration and resource materials
  • General ship upkeep and maintenance

You pay for yourself

  • Travel between your home country and the outreach country
  • Your visa for the outreach country
  • Personal spending money
  • Immunizations and malaria prophylaxis
  • Personal travel insurance and professional indemnity insurance
  • Any extra team items you'd like to buy

For current outreach pricing and dates, email medicalvolunteers@ywamships.net (medical) or join-us@ywamships.net (marine and general).

Life on board

Living in close community is new for some people, and we work together to make it a good experience. Here's what to expect.

Getting there
Outreaches hub through Port Moresby, the capital. The registrar tells you your destination and airport before you book, and a YWAM representative meets you when you land and drives you to the ship.
Your cabin
Cabins are shared with other volunteers. Married couples and families get private cabins whenever possible. The stairs are steep and the quarters compact, yet comfortable.
A clinic week
Clinics usually run five days a week. Sundays are often a day off, with a chance to join a local church service. Everyone helps with housekeeping, like dinner prep or clean-up.
Training and debrief
Before clinics begin you're trained in on-board safety and national medical standards. Each outreach ends with reflection and celebration with the ship community.
Food
The galley cooks balanced meals with what's available in PNG. Fresh produce can be limited, and we can't cater for specific diets or dietary restrictions.
Climate
Hot and humid ashore, cool at night on board thanks to air conditioning. Pack for both.
Faith
YWAM is a Christian mission and Papua New Guinea is a predominantly Christian nation. There's worship and prayer on board and on outreach, and you're invited to pray with the patients you serve.
Who leads
The ship coordinator leads each outreach, the medical coordinator supports the clinic leaders, and the captain is responsible for navigation and safety on board.
Flexibility
Papua New Guinea calls itself “the land of the unexpected”. Schedules and outreach locations can change at short notice, so come ready to be flexible.

Questions volunteers ask

Is YWAM Ships Kona a Christian organization?

Yes. YWAM is a Christian mission organization, and Papua New Guinea is a predominantly Christian nation. On board there are times of worship and prayer, and you're invited to pray with the patients you serve. We work closely with local churches, which are often the main health care providers.

How much does it cost to volunteer?

The application fee is $50, plus a 2.3% surcharge. Each outreach also has a fee that covers your food, cabin, airport transport, uniform shirt, materials and the ship's upkeep. Flights, visas, insurance, immunizations and spending money are yours to cover.

For current pricing, email medicalvolunteers@ywamships.net for medical outreaches or join-us@ywamships.net for marine and general volunteering.

How long can I serve?

Medical outreaches last about two weeks each. You're welcome to register for more than one, or give us a range of dates. We'll try to give you your first choice, but because of high interest we can't always guarantee extra outreaches.

General volunteers serve in Kona for four weeks to three months. Long-term staff positions are open to DTS graduates.

Can I bring my spouse or children?

Married couples and families get private cabins whenever possible. The ship isn't suitable for children under 6, and anyone traveling to Papua New Guinea alone must be at least 18.

Can we come as a team?

We love teams! We often organize outreaches for churches or business teams at our land locations or on board the ship. Let us know well in advance, as team spots on board fill quickly.

Apply as a team

Do I need a visa?

The visa for your outreach country isn't covered by the outreach fee, but we help medical volunteers with visas, permits and other field requirements. If you're coming to Port YWAM in Kona from outside the US, our visa team can tell you whether you need a visa or can travel on an ESTA.

Email visa@ywamships.net, and don't buy flights until your visa is in place.

What about insurance and vaccinations?

Personal travel insurance, professional indemnity insurance, immunizations and malaria prophylaxis aren't covered by the outreach fee, so plan for them before you go.

What if I need to cancel?

Tell us as soon as you can, as others may be on the waiting list. Cancellation details and fees are in your Preparing to Come pack.
