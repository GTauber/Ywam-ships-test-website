Serving the isolated.

We're a volunteer Christian mission based in Kona. We train young people to know God and make Him known, then sail the m/v YWAM Liberty to islands and shoreline villages that no road or runway reaches.

YWAM Ships Kona exists to serve isolated islands and remote shoreline villages through health and healing services.

  • We work with local governments

    We go by invitation only. We partner with local authorities and build long-term, sustainable partnerships.

  • We provide free medical care

    We sail to isolated places, set up clinics ashore and do surgeries on board, all for free.

  • 100% of our staff are volunteers

    From our founder to our janitor, no one here takes a salary. Every staff member raises their own support.

936 islands in the Pacific have no airport, no dock, and no road or bridge joining them.

It can take weeks to sail to them across the Pacific, the ocean we call the liquid continent. For these villages a ship isn't just a good idea. It's the only option.

The Big Island of Hawaiʻi sits in the heart of that ocean, ideally placed for this work. It's where we train, and where our teams set out from.

From a pier in Kona to a dock in Madang

Our story starts almost two centuries before we did, with a ship that anchored across the street from what is now our front door.

The ships before us

1820 to 1983

  1. 1820

    The Thaddeus anchors at Kailua

    After about 164 days at sea from Boston, the brig brings the first Protestant missionaries to Hawaiʻi. Port YWAM sits across the street from that pier, beside Mokuaikaua, Hawaiʻi's first church.

  2. 1956

    A vision of waves

    Loren Cunningham, 20, has a vision in the Bahamas: a map of the world that “was alive and moving!”, with waves rolling onto every continent and turning into young people going out as missionaries.

  3. 1960

    Youth With A Mission begins

    Loren founds YWAM after a youth trip to Hawaiʻi. Three years later, YWAM sends its first teams to Hawaiʻi.

  4. 1978

    A university in Kona, and a first mercy ship

    Loren Cunningham and Howard Malmstadt found Pacific & Asia Christian University in Kailua-Kona, now the University of the Nations. The same year, Don and Deyon Stephens buy an Italian liner from a Greek scrapyard and rename her Anastasis, YWAM's first mercy ship. Ships and the university have worked as twins ever since. (Mercy Ships has been a separate organization since 2003.)

  5. 1983

    Brett and Karen Curtis come aboard

    The Anastasis calls at Tauranga, New Zealand, the young couple's home port, and they join YWAM's ships. Brett would go on to found YWAM Ships Kona.

YWAM Ships Kona

2011 to today

  1. 2011

    We begin in California

    YWAM Ships Orange County is incorporated on August 26. It is still our legal name; we do business as YWAM Ships Kona.

  2. 2013

    A new name and a home in Kona

    On July 23 we become YWAM Ships Kona. Port YWAM opens in the former Hukilau Hotel on Aliʻi Drive, and in September our first School of Navigation and Seamanship starts with nearly 60 students.

  3. 2014

    An invitation from the Marshall Islands

    In Majuro, President Christopher Loeak comes aboard and invites us for ten years. That year our teams give Lib Island its first vaccinations in a decade, and with SIL and Wycliffe carry the first complete Bible to Kapingamarangi.

  4. 2015

    The Pacific Link

    YWAM Medical Ships Australia gives us the m/v Pacific Link, with 50 beds and an operating room. From October she serves in Papua New Guinea.

  5. 2016

    Five years for Papua New Guinea

    We commit five years to PNG. The Pacific Link serves Madang and East Sepik, where clinics run 85 miles up the Sepik River, and our Cloud Nine partnership begins in the Marshall Islands.

  6. 2017

    L'Astrolabe becomes the Liberty

    A French polar supply ship joins YWAM and is renamed YWAM Liberty. In June she sails from Queensland through the Great Barrier Reef on her maiden voyage to Papua New Guinea.

  7. 2018

    The baton passes

    At Lae, the Pacific Link retires after 25 years in YWAM and the Liberty is commissioned. Her first deployment, at President John Momis's invitation, is to Bougainville: 20,857 medical services in 53 villages.

  8. 2019

    Stranded, but not stopped

    In Bougainville her main engines are taken apart down to the crankshaft for inspection. Then COVID-19 closes borders, and the Liberty waits out the pandemic laid up in Port Moresby.

  9. 2022

    Waiting on parts

    Papua New Guinea reopens in February, but engine parts take nearly nine months to arrive. In Kona that spring, we buy Port YWAM, the old hotel we had been renting.

  10. 2023

    Madang becomes home port

    We sign a 50-year lease on a dock and warehouse in Madang that once belonged to the Lutheran Shipping Company.

  11. 2024

    Back at sea

    In November the Liberty passes her sea trials, with propulsion, safety drills and communications all tested at sea.

  12. 2025

    Home in Madang

    The Liberty arrives in Madang in January. Her teams run clinics along the Rai Coast and on Karkar and Manam, Bible translators live aboard while they record Scripture in their own languages, and by the end of the year she has provided more than 20,000 medical services.

  13. 2026

    Serving Madang town

    At Modilon General Hospital's request, outreach voyages are paused while the Liberty supports health services in Madang town. In February the chief executive of the Madang Provincial Health Authority called the ship “an extension” of the province's health system.

For 29 years she was L'Astrolabe, the French polar supply ship that sailed from Hobart, Tasmania, to Dumont d'Urville station in Antarctica.

In March 2017 her owner, P&O Maritime, valued her at $11.6 million and sold her to YWAM for $300,000, enough to cover parts, fuel and the trip north. YWAM Norway and YWAM Medical Ships Australia helped make it happen.

We named her Liberty to honor her French history, and for people living in bondage: “where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty” (2 Corinthians 3:17).

About a third of her crew are Papua New Guineans, many of them trained at PNG Maritime College in Madang.

The only ship in the YWAM fleet that carries containers

Each shipping container is a room fitted out for one job, and friends of the ship in different countries each took one on.

  • Dental clinic Norway
  • TB and X-ray lab Canada
  • Eye surgery Germany
  • Pharmacy Hawaiʻi
  • Bible translation recording studio The first of its kind

The plan has room for more: classrooms, libraries, woodshops and birthing suites.

Where we serve

Most of our work today happens on one stretch of coast in Papua New Guinea. We train in Kona, and partners carry the work on in the Marshall Islands.

Madang, Papua New Guinea

Since January 2025 the Liberty has been based in Madang, in PNG's Momase region, where we hold a 50-year lease on a dock and warehouse. From here her teams have run clinics along the Rai Coast and out to the islands, often with Bible translators living aboard.

As of April 2026, outreach voyages are paused while the Liberty supports health services in Madang town, at the request of Modilon General Hospital.

  • Madang

    The Liberty's home port. In 2025 our medical team ran workshops with nurses and clinical staff at Modilon General Hospital.

  • Rai Coast

    The Liberty's first outreach from Madang, in early 2025. About 2,000 people were waiting before 8 a.m.; some had walked for three days.

  • Karkar Island

    At a Jesus Film screening at Miak Health Centre, 200 to 300 people came, and Pullung heard the Lord's Prayer in Waskia, his own language, for the first time.

  • Manam Island

    Bible translators worked here at Pentecost 2025, while the volcano smoked by day and glowed red at night.

  • Bunabun

    In 2025, the Liberty's first visit in more than two years, after tribal fighting.

  • Long Island and Bagabag

    Served by the Pacific Link in 2016, before the Liberty came.

The Marshall Islands

In 2014 President Christopher Loeak invited us for ten years. Today our partners Scott and Katie Suderno of Changing Focus Ministries sail the Cloud Nine, a sixty-foot ketch, from Ebeye on three-week trips to the remote western atolls. Ebeye is also an outreach destination for many of our students. It takes fourteen days to sail there from Kona.

Kailua-Kona, Hawaiʻi

Port YWAM is our training and field support base, a former hotel on Aliʻi Drive across from Kailua Pier and a mile from our twin, the University of the Nations Kona. Our Discipleship Training Schools begin here, and our volunteers give more than 30,000 hours a year to the Kona community.

Train with us in Kona

Over the years our teams have also served in Bougainville, East and West New Britain, East Sepik and the Western Highlands of PNG, and Discipleship Training School outreach teams travel across the Pacific and beyond.

Who leads us

Like everyone here, our leaders are volunteers who raise their own support.

Brett and Karen Curtis

Brett is our founder, president and CEO. Together they direct Port YWAM.

They began with YWAM's ships in 1983, when the hospital ship Anastasis called at their home port of Tauranga, New Zealand. Several decades, multiple vessels and five children later, they have served in more than seventy nations on six continents.

What we believe

We're part of Youth With A Mission, a global, volunteer-based, non-denominational Christian movement in more than 1,200 locations and 180 countries.

The Christian Magna Carta

YWAM Ships Kona affirms the Christian Magna Carta, which describes the following basic rights as implicit in the Gospel. Everyone on earth has the right to:

  • Hear and understand the Gospel of Jesus Christ.
  • Have a Bible available in their own language.
  • Have a Christian fellowship available nearby, to be able to meet for fellowship regularly each week, and to have biblical teaching and worship with others in the Body of Christ.
  • Have a Christian education available for their children.
  • Have the necessities of life: food, water, clothing, shelter and healthcare.
  • Lead a productive life with spiritual, mental, social, emotional and physical fulfillment.

How YWAM is organized

Every YWAM entity shares one faith and one set of values, but each is legally and financially separate, with its own board of directors and its own independent audits. We work closely with the University of the Nations Kona as twin ministries, but we are separate organizations.

All our volunteers are expected to follow our code of conduct, in effect since April 2015.
