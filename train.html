Know God. Make Him known.

A Discipleship Training School is where everyone in YWAM starts. It's five to six months that take you deeper with God and closer to your calling.

A DTS is for anyone who longs to follow Jesus in new ways, with a different perspective. You'll learn from experienced missionaries in a missions-focused community, discover your passions, and find your part in God's purposes for the world.

Bring what's in your hands. From construction to medical work to creative arts, there's room to use it. After you graduate you can serve with YWAM in more than 1,400 locations around the world, or keep going with a second-level course here in Kona.

  1. Lecture phase

    12 weeks

    Kona, Hawaiʻi

    Classroom learning, community and heart change. Every week a different speaker teaches on topics like hearing God's voice, the Father heart of God, the Holy Spirit and spiritual warfare. You'll also serve in community outreach and help run the base.

  2. Outreach phase

    8 to 12 weeks

    Overseas, with your team

    The classroom becomes practical. You serve alongside local believers and experienced missionaries, sharing the love of Jesus in another culture. Locations are usually announced in the first or second week of lecture phase.

  3. Graduation

    1 to 2 weeks

    Back in Kona

    Your team comes home to Kona to finish the school together.

Discovering the ancient pathways of the Pacific.

Aloha DTS

Fishers of men.

Hawaiʻi, the Pacific, Asia and beyond

In Hawaiian, holoholo means "to go fishing". Aloha DTS, also called Holoholo, puts Jesus' call to be fishers of men (Matthew 4:19) at its heart. Rooted in the Polynesian culture of Hawaiʻi, with staff shaped by both Hawaiian and Asian cultures, it values living in community, championing local people and learning from the cultures it serves. Holoholo is pioneering a new base in the Philippines and serves in places like Thailand and the Pacific. Creative arts are part of the mission: dance, song, art and instruments.

And if it's not something you're gifted in, no worries. We all started from scratch.

Wayfinders DTS

Live a life of extraordinary adventure with Jesus.

Kona, Hawaiʻi, the islands and beyond

Jesus said, "I am the Way and the Truth and the Life." Wayfinders takes its name from hoʻokele, the Hawaiian word for navigation or wayfinding, and from the ancient Polynesian navigators who crossed the vast Pacific, finding new islands and building communities. Everything flows from closeness with God. This DTS is geared toward hands-on outreach, so bring your skills: the team will help you use them for the kingdom.

If Jesus is the Way, then we are those who will stop at nothing to find it—Wayfinders.

Twelve weeks on Aliʻi Drive

Port YWAM is a 1950s hotel in downtown Kailua-Kona, reused as our training base. Hawaiian families knew it as the Hukilau Hotel. Today it sleeps 200 and has five classrooms, across the street from the ocean.

It sits across from the pier where the first missionaries to Hawaiʻi landed in 1820, next door to Mokuaikaua, Hawaiʻi's first church, and one mile from the University of the Nations Kona campus, our twin in ministry.

Your tuition covers room and board and all course transport: shuttles to and from the airport and the University of the Nations, weekly shopping and course outings. You won't need a car.

How to apply

Signing up takes 5 to 10 minutes. Use the same email address all the way through, and our admissions team will help if anything gets stuck.

  1. Sign up

    Fill in the online application for the school you've chosen. We process it within 48 hours.

  2. Pay the application fee

    Once we've reviewed your application, we email you a link to pay the $75 fee, plus a 2.3% surcharge. It secures your spot and your rides to and from Kona airport.

  3. Enroll

    Send your enrollment details and the forms below. If you need a US visa, our visa team will contact you.

  4. Arrive

    You fly in to Kona and we pick you up.

Questions people ask

Can't find yours? Email registrar@ywamships.net or call +1 (808) 757-9150.

How much does a DTS cost?

Most of our DTS cost $10,100, which covers room and board, tuition and airfare. Ring of Fire and Aloha DTS cost $11,100.

The actual cost can change with your outreach destination or unexpected travel costs. If it does, we'll tell you promptly.

Can people give toward my DTS and get a tax receipt?

No. DTS fees pay for educational services, so they aren't a charitable gift and don't qualify as a tax-deductible donation.

When do schools start?

Every quarter: January, April, July and September. In some quarters two DTS run at the same time, each with its own mission focus.

Where will I go on outreach?

Outreach locations are usually announced in the first or second week of lecture phase. Past teams have served in Papua New Guinea, Micronesia and the Pacific, Panama, Haiti and the Caribbean, and Cambodia.

How much spending money should I bring?

It depends on you. A frugal student may only need $20 a week for laundry and personal things. If you'd like to eat out, see a movie or explore Hawaiʻi, plan on $100 to $150 a week.

What's the weather like?

Warm all year. In February and March the average high is 82°F (28°C) and the low about 66°F (19°C). In October and November it's 86°F (30°C) and 71°F (21°C). Humidity is usually between 50% and 70%.

Can my parents visit?

Yes, we place a high value on family. DTS has a full schedule, so plan the timing with your course leader. Visitors are welcome to stay with us if rooms are free: email hospitality@ywamships.net.

Can you cater for dietary restrictions?

Not at the moment. If you have dietary restrictions, you'll provide and prepare your own meals. The shared galley kitchen has a microwave, toaster oven, electric kettle and air fryer you're welcome to use.

How old do I need to be?

The minimum age for a DTS is 18.

Who teaches in lecture phase?

Speakers change each week. In the past we've been honored to welcome Darlene Cunningham, Brett and Karen Curtis, Danny Lehman, David Hamilton, Jonathan Spainhour, Jim Walker, Joseph Avakian and many others.

What should I pack?

Our staff made a video about it: What to pack for DTS, part 1 (YouTube).

Can I take college classes during the Bible Core Course?

BCC is three months to study the living word of God and let Him work in your life. We recommend you don't take on extra work, so you get the most out of that time.
